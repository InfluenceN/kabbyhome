# Amber Leaf product discovery and customization update

The existing static website, brand typography, kitchen photography, resources and SEO routes remain in place. The homepage now follows the nine-section hierarchy in the brief. Primary navigation prioritizes cabinets, finishes, accessories, projects, process and showroom coverage; guides and comparisons remain secondary.

The reusable catalog contains 11 clearly labeled planning models across base, wall and tall cabinets. Live filters intersect dimensions, style, finish, material, price status and accessory fit. URL parameters preserve filters on return and carry selected dimensions, finish and storage into customization. Four finish identities are shared across the catalog, menu, homepage and configurator. Legacy Olive Modern and Classic White links have canonical, noindex HTML redirects to Charcoal Gray and Soft White; these are browser redirects, not server-level HTTP 301 responses.

The six-stage configurator supports cabinet type, preset dimensions or a custom-size inquiry, separate door and box materials, compatible storage, editable multi-cabinet projects and essential quote contact details. Drafts save locally; customers can resume or start fresh. Selections do not reload the page. Stage transitions scroll to the stage heading. Downloadable text references include configurations, scope, notes and itemized price status.

Pricing is “Quote required” throughout the new journey. No factory pricebook has been supplied, so numeric budget comparisons and calculated price differences cannot be provided honestly. Customer-entered budget preferences in the kitchen survey remain preferences, not product prices. Quote preparation opens a confirmation page with a reviewable brief, a download and an email handoff. It does not submit to a backend, upload files, send an email, take payment or place an order.

Accessory rules reject incompatible prototype types and widths and suggest an alternative filtered catalog. These are planning rules only; internal clearance, mechanisms, mounting, plumbing and actual factory SKUs require approval before manufacturing. Changing a width removes storage selections that no longer fit the rules.

Naperville retains the drag-based before/after comparison and dated before image. It is explicitly a fictional concept. Other location pages use distinct planning prompts and honest coverage/contact information rather than pretending that generated photos document local installations. These pages remain noindex until verified local evidence is available.

Factory SKU inventory and manufacturable dimensions; approved door/box specifications; accessory SKU clearance and compatibility tables; actual product photographs; verified pricebook and finish/accessory allowances; measurement, assembly, delivery and installation responsibilities; warranty and quality-check documentation; verified customer projects and permitted prices/reviews; actual showroom photos, parking/accessibility and consultation policy.
